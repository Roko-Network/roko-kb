# Business Meeting Summary

**Date:** March 24, 2026

---

## Funding and Deliverables Focus

The meeting opened with the business team seeking a clear, high-level set of deliverables for the coming month beyond the central priority of funding. The team acknowledged that members are currently working in separate zones and projects until milestones — mainnet deployment, validator onboarding documentation, and a clear path to recurring revenue — are met, at which point a formal business plan for the network can be formalized. An early invoicing and Stripe setup was noted as newly operational, with some initial inbound interest already arriving.

## Pitch Materials as Top Priority

When asked to rank priorities, the team placed pitch decks and pitch material first, noting that the technology spans multiple use cases and sectors that could each be pitched to both technical and non-technical audiences. A clear articulation from the L1 team on how the network reaches its milestones was identified as a needed input for the pitch materials.

## Contributor Tracking and Grants Program

A second priority discussed was a system to track meaningful contributions from people outside the core team. The team has a rough grants-program outline that could be refreshed with current direction now that the L1 architecture is clearer. The concept involves a dedicated grants subdomain or page, a way to authenticate contributors by connecting a GitHub identity to a web3 wallet, and issuing a non-transferable on-chain asset to backtrack contributions to the DAO. This was framed as also tying into governance and tokenomics through how participation maps to distributed power. Decentralized-identity approaches were discussed as eventually unavoidable for understanding the contribution landscape, with an encrypted, user-held data model where the system can validate asset holdings without exposing unencrypted data.

## Payment Rails and Metering

The team discussed ongoing work on payment metering and an HTTP 402 / X402 validator setup using a third-party RPC provider key, giving the team practical experience taking in funds and enabling contribution in a test setting before generalizing it to a network-specific, white-label version. The team agreed to sit down and architect a viable product for contributor onboarding, metering of inference endpoints, and security review, targeting a working prototype by the end of April.

## DAO Research via Auto-Research Tooling

A proposed near-term task was using the auto-research capability of the agent tooling to collect and distill best practices from the documentation, blog posts, and governance materials of the top DAOs by total value locked. One participant had already produced a markdown distillation covering roughly the top seventy DAOs by TVL, observing that quality of insight degrades further down the list and that the value lies in identifying pitfalls in smaller-to-mid-cap protocols while using the largest DAOs as guiding references. The team discussed building a constrained, application-specific research pattern wrapped around scheduled jobs — referencing a tracking document of what has and has not been researched, spinning up memory and summary components, and running steps sequentially and in parallel toward defined completion metrics — and using the DAO research example as a ground-truth test harness while guarding against reward hacking. Document sharing during the call used a peer-to-peer file transfer tool that the team found notably useful.

## Geospatial and Defense Use Cases

A substantial portion of the call explored a geospatial application area. The team discussed a low-cost RTK-style device — described as roughly a Raspberry Pi-class board with cellular connectivity — that, combined with the network's high-fidelity timing, could turn ordinary phones into accurate survey instruments and support a training-data acquisition marketplace. Public-facing applications discussed included low-cost municipal surveying. A related concept was pairwise distance estimation: because time-protocol packets exchanged between peers accumulate a minimum observed one-way delay that converges toward true propagation delay, the mesh could in principle build a pairwise distance matrix and, at sufficient node density, triangulate device positions — effectively a positioning capability, with the team noting this also raises surveillance considerations.

For defense, the team discussed how field assets with limited infrastructure could attest their presence and coordinate cascading actions simply by signing a timestamped transaction tied to a public key, without deploying sophisticated infrastructure. A point of debate was whether a public, trustless mesh is suitable for defense use, given that participating nodes could expose position information; one view held that a separated, military-only private network might be needed for defense customers, while another argued the core value of the network is precisely that no single nation-state controls it, providing trustless time even if individual nodes are compromised. The team agreed the public and defense threads are distinct, and that defense framing and customer discovery would benefit from contacts with relevant domain experience. The team noted that articles drafted on these topics still require refinement by someone with genuine military-domain experience.
