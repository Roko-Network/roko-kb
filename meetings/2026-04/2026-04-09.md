# Development Meeting Summary

**Date:** April 9, 2026

---

## Wallet and Faucet Development

Work has begun on wallet integration directed at the Roko chain, along with a faucet site, building on the validator and documentation work the broader team has produced. The wallet effort draws on the Fortemi data wallet, whose backup, export, and data-sharding capabilities are built on a PKCS#12 key store that creates a wallet behind the scenes when backup encryption is enabled. The current implementation is generic PKCS#12 and is being adapted to substrate while remaining modular enough to plug into multiple chains. The team framed this as delivering on the original promise of NFTs — licensing and access control over decentralized storage — including the confederated-storage model that brings businesses in, without adopting the NFT label.

## Mesh Layer Hardening

The protocol engineer reported small tweaks to the mesh layer prompted by an incident in which a newly launched validator, affected by a port-forwarding issue on the operator's side, briefly connected to a peer and left stale temporal data that drained that peer's reputation over time. A filter now activates within roughly ten seconds to clean up such stale data, and a small attack scenario exercising that vector was tested and confirmed no longer effective. Substrate-related features are also being added to the block explorer.

## Network Documentation

A new half-technical document describing the Roko network is in progress. The author flagged a desire to include the open question of how to reward validators for timestamping — a capability not yet implemented because the best mechanism has not been settled — so the team can think through it collectively.

## Tokenomics and Economic Model

The bulk of the meeting was a wide-ranging tokenomics discussion, treated explicitly as a brainstorm rather than settled policy. The team weighed a two-token model separating a governance/DAO token from the chain's native operational coin, drawing an analogy to corporate structures where the board is deliberately separate from management. Under the model discussed, validators would lock a governance token to participate, while the native coin would be kept relatively stable and inexpensive to keep it flowing freely for services. The intent is to unhook the network's intrinsic equitable value (which pays dividends) from its operational cost, so that volatility in one does not destabilize the other.

The team also discussed a possible migration away from the existing Ethereum-based token, noting the present moment — with a holder base in the low thousands and limited on-chain liquidity tooling — as the least disruptive time to do so if it were ever undertaken. Such a migration was treated as not on the table until DeFi, a DEX, and bridge basics are in place, and several participants flagged legal review and a careful dilution strategy as prerequisites. The treasury was noted to still hold roughly 30–40% of supply, which could provide runway for emissions. A staking model in which the governance token is delegated to validators to earn chain emissions was discussed, along with a phased "dam" liquidity-release concept designed to spin up circulating flow gradually rather than dumping value onto the market.

## Enterprise Positioning

The team discussed positioning the network for enterprise customers, framing it as an alternative to traditional cloud providers where the chain's emissions and operation could offset much of a customer's operating cost after their up-front hardware expenditure. The group acknowledged the pricing must remain within a competitive range of mainstream cloud providers to be credible. A Monte Carlo simulation setup was offered as a tool to model the economics and product additions more rigorously.
