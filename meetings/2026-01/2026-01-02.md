# Strategy Meeting Summary

**Date:** January 2, 2026

---

## Funding Approach & Investor Profile

The group met to align on fundraising strategy ahead of preparing pitch materials for investors. The discussion centered on identifying a first commercial vertical and producing investor-ready decks. For traditional family offices and private investors, a conventional structured pitch was favored — problem, solution, market sizing, and a multi-year growth narrative with an emphasis on return and condensed time horizons. Seed interest discussed to date has come from private individuals rather than institutional offices, with rough figures in the low single-digit millions under consideration. The group noted that academic and scientific funding sources were a distinct and appealing path worth a separate deck, given the network's potential to advance research applications.

## Timeline Clarification

The team clarified that the first-quarter milestone targets testnet, not mainnet. Mainnet readiness was discussed as contingent on infrastructure buildout, with an earliest realistic target of mid-year. All testing and hardware provisioning must be completed on testnet before any mainnet transition is attempted.

## First Vertical: AI Agent Accountability

The leading candidate for an initial vertical is AI agent auditability and accountability, motivated by emerging regulatory pressure requiring AI agents to demonstrate and prove their decision-making. The concept involves middleware that writes AI agent actions on-chain with cryptographic timestamps and ordering, producing an immutable, replayable audit trail. Recruiting and talent-acquisition platforms were cited as an accessible entry point given existing domain relationships, with the possibility of onboarding an end client rather than requiring a major platform vendor to integrate first. The team agreed the technical feasibility and end-to-end transaction flow should be reviewed with the lead engineer before finalizing the pitch.

## Platform vs. Application Architecture

A recurring theme was the distinction between Roko as foundational network infrastructure and the applications built on top of it. The group aligned on treating the network as open, decentralized routing-and-timestamp infrastructure — analogized to an open-source operating system — while individual applications layered on top may be proprietary. This framing avoids pigeonholing the network into a single use case while allowing specific verticals to be monetized. The proposed AI accountability product was characterized as one application among many that could run on the network and drive transaction value back to it.

## Investment Structuring & Governance

The discussion explored how investment would flow given the project's DAO structure. Because the DAO is not yet positioned to accept investment directly or manage distributions, a structure was proposed in which a partner application entity serves as the custodian of investment, manages the relationship with investors, and allocates funds into network development through the DAO's grant mechanism. This creates a buffer between investors and the DAO while keeping application ownership with its originators. The group emphasized that any structure must preserve room for a decentralized approach and that this should be articulated clearly in the pitch. Treasury-management tooling for the DAO, raised previously, was noted as a future step deferred while funds have been directed at building.

## Infrastructure Reality

The team reviewed the practical infrastructure requirements behind going to mainnet versus standing up dedicated service for a single application. Public mainnet was estimated to require a substantial infrastructure investment in the low millions, whereas private, dedicated servers to run a single partner application could be far more manageable and stood up sooner. The architecture envisions both a public network service and an enterprise/corporate service tier, reflected in how infrastructure is allocated. Hardware-based gateways (edge devices providing precise timing) were described as necessary to obtain the full benefits of the technology, distinguishing the offering from free, cloud-based network time. The discussion also touched on geographic distribution of timing beacons and the dependency on specialized timing-hardware vendors.
