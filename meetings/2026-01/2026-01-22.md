# Strategy Meeting Summary

**Date:** January 22, 2026

---

## Token Model and Settlement Mechanics

The team continued discussing how a credit-style token might work for paying for validator and timing services. A preferred direction is a familiar "preload and drain" experience — users load funds (for example, a stablecoin) that translate into credits used against utilization of validators and their timing or notary-type services, with automatic top-up. The appeal is that this model is familiar to users and front-loads the network with funds even before they are consumed.

A more experimental idea was raised: rather than a token users hold long-term, the settlement credit could be ephemeral — existing on-chain only during the course of a transaction, functioning more like a flash-loan-style settlement contract. The goal would be to shield service providers from market-price volatility on the spot compute market so that operators are not exposed to losses and are not discouraged from participating. The credits would act as transient units of account that facilitate the underlying economics without being something participants must consciously hold. The team agreed this concept was worth simulating and experimenting with, while acknowledging that a blockchain system inevitably requires the token to be part of the economic loop.

## Roko TV and Community Streaming

An initiative to build out a streaming presence — informally "Roko TV" — was announced. A Twitch account is being tested as the basis for a channel, with the goal of running continuous content (recorded meetings, hangouts, and project updates) to advertise the project and engage the community. The infrastructure is now in place to record a call and publish it online within a couple of hours, and the intent is to move toward a rotating list of videos that resembles a real TV channel, with bumpers and more professional production over time. The team discussed posting meeting recordings, audio, and transcripts publicly, pending member comfort.

## Immersive and Interactive Demos

Several creative engagement ideas were explored, including piping live audio/video streams into a 3D web environment (a "noclip" observability platform) with spatial audio, allowing participants to gather virtually and watch streams together. The concept extended to bringing custom 3D models — such as project hardware — into a shared virtual lab space for people to inspect. The broader theme was that the project is multi-phasic, letting the team engage different audiences (Web3, industrial automation, AI workflows, hardware) through whichever facet interests them, and that multimedia engagement is a critical way to build the audience the project needs.

## Hardware Server Requirements

The team prepared for an upcoming hardware-requirements meeting with the timing-hardware partner. The architecture keeps the time appliances/servers running separately from the validator computer, positioned as close to the time source as possible. The benchmark configuration discussed is a blade enclosure occupying 6U of rack space and holding up to ten individual systems, providing density advantages over standard servers. The plan considered partially filling these with PPS cards and optional GPUs, with enough overhead to run roughly two nodes per machine plus additional workloads (such as Polkadot or bridges). This was framed as an uncompromising starting position from which trade-offs would be made, and the partner is expected to bring concrete numbers to the follow-up meeting.

## Community Hardware Involvement

The team discussed low-cost hardware projects as an on-ramp for community members — accessible, inexpensive devices that introduce people to embedded systems and tie into the broader platform, even when not directly timing-validator hardware. The goal is to get people involved and engaged through hands-on, approachable projects.
