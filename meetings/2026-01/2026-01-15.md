# Development Meeting Summary

**Date:** January 15, 2026

---

## The Fee Problem

This session presented a proposed solution to the standing question of how to charge for transactions on a time-ordered chain. Because temporal ordering removes the usual incentive for users to compete on fees, the network loses the open-market mechanism that normally prevents transaction flooding, protects storage and memory, and lets fees rise organically under congestion. The challenge framed was how to reintroduce a market for a scarce resource without reintroducing fee-based ordering, and to do so via the shortest path back to open-market price discovery.

## Two-Part Pricing Proposal

The proposal splits pricing into two components. The first is a static (optionally semi-dynamic) execution fee paid in Roko, suggested to be set relatively high but left open for discussion. The second, and the focus of the presentation, is a temporal access fee. Temporal ordering itself remains fee-independent, but access to temporal transactions would be gated by a separate mechanism described below.

## Time Credits Token

The central idea is a new fungible utility token — referred to as time credits — that must be consumed (burned) to create a temporal transaction, on a one-credit-per-transaction basis. Because the credits are burned on use, the supply is non-inflationary and the token does not touch the ordering system. Crucially, the credits would be tradable on an open market, which restores open-market price discovery: instead of per-block fee competition, price discovery happens continuously through trading of credits on a DEX. Users would pre-purchase temporal bandwidth, which is less reactive than a live fee but allows usage even during congestion. The token was characterized as a pure utility token, which was seen as advantageous from a regulatory standpoint, and the discussion acknowledged it would be a third token alongside Roko and Power Roko.

## Credit Minting and Validator Incentives

It was proposed that validators mint the credits as they produce time beacons — work for which they are currently not rewarded. This would give validators a second reward stream on top of block rewards and would make beacon production a core, incentivized activity. Credit issuance would scale with the network: as more validators join, more credits are minted and more temporal transactions can be handled, giving the system a direct scalability formula. A primary market (validators selling credits to claim their reward) and a secondary market (traders speculating on network usage) were both described. Validators could also sell credits directly to their own clients as subscriptions, effectively becoming time-service providers and gateways into the network, with the option to pre-sell future credits.

## Credit Decay

A final idea raised was making credits decay over time, similar to a rebasing token whose balance reduces gradually. Decay would discourage indefinite hoarding and shorten the trading loop, and could be tuned from slow (decaying over a year) to fast (decaying within roughly a week). Linear decay was suggested as preferable, with a credit's value sliding smoothly while still requiring a whole credit to create a temporal transaction. The team agreed the proposal was a promising first pass to be considered further, and that tokenizing transactions appears to be the most elegant path to an open market for this kind of system.

## Testnet Update

On the technical side, two configuration-level fixes were made on-chain and the Docker image was rebuilt. Using the binary-and-script path, a new validator was created through the Roko admin interface and successfully joined the network, producing blocks within minutes. The testnet was restarted with a shortened epoch to allow faster validator inclusion. Validator onboarding on testnet is now considered straightforward via the two-step process. Work on the reward tracker was paused while the fee work and beacon-related tasks took priority.
