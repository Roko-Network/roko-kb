# Strategy Meeting Summary

**Date:** December 30, 2025

---

## Funding Outlook & Team Morale

The meeting opened with an acknowledgment that realizing funding will take time and a "dirty grind," paired with concern that team members have bills to pay during the wait. The group affirmed a shared commitment to keep momentum, keep heads down, and work together to assemble a fundable pitch, noting that once investors come on board the direction will be set.

## Testnet & Infrastructure Status

The infrastructure lead reported progress toward bringing the synchronized clock network online, targeting that week. The systems have been rebuilt across the lead's network, another thorough security audit completed, and the VPN infrastructure to securely bootstrap and distribute the time signal is under construction (with a mesh-VPN tool praised as a significant help). Public-facing repositories were planned for release within a couple of weeks of testnet going live, with a deliberate hold on publishing code immediately in case testing surfaces issues. Source code deployment to machines was expected to begin within 24 hours. The team also planned a hands-on session to help a less technical member get their hardware (a single-board computer) set up and onto the network — part of broader work on making onboarding easier for users who need extra help.

## Structuring the Pitch: Core vs. Apps

The advisor leading the investor work outlined the need to define "Roko Core" versus "Roko Apps," and to identify which capabilities are core Roko versus services consumed from partners. The group agreed to dedicate a focused follow-up session (targeted for Friday) to developing a small number of vertical strategies and a roadmap for the next 18–24 months, framed so investors — whether family offices or institutions — can understand a crisp core product. Several organizing themes were identified: the core network functionality and, critically, Roko's fee structure (flagged as the biggest open gap); strategic partnerships such as ISP providers; and a grant-funding section to seed application development. A clarification was also made that a payment on-ramp service raised previously was a partner suggestion for fiat/crypto onboarding, distinct from the more sensitive investment vehicle the fund-management partner is developing for family offices, which remains under NDA.

## Timing Partner: Service vs. Ingestion

A substantive debate addressed how central the London timing partner should be to Roko's offering. The advisor questioned whether Roko needs to resell PTP timing as a service to others, suggesting the partner may be better used as an ingestion tool and that Roko's core product is better described as deterministic ordering, settlement, and auditable execution rather than timing per se. The team explained the partner's practical role: potentially providing master-clock infrastructure so Roko can focus on the edge (avoiding a large self-managed core investment), and supplying the certification and international regional certifications that make a PTP service legitimate under regulatory standards — services that command significant annual cost and require specialized engineers. Some equipment purchases already include a limited allotment of the partner's development time. The team noted the partner is among very few organizations worldwide offering a decentralized timing-network approach, raising a vendor-dependency consideration worth examining.

## Core Differentiator: Auditability Over Timing

The discussion converged on the view that Roko's highest-value proposition may be ordering, settlement, and auditable execution rather than timing alone. The advisor framed this against existing cloud-compute offerings: work written immutably to the blockchain, with validators concurring, removes the need for after-the-fact auditing — a potential "game changer" that could displace traditional audit work. The team discussed that achieving institutional adoption may hinge on developing an amendment to the established PTP standard to legitimize the blockchain-backed automated process, and that investor interest could help drive that certification effort. An alternative path was raised: first determine how much compliance can be fully automated under existing amendments, potentially needing only a certified human sign-off at specific points rather than a new amendment.

## Privacy-Preserving Attestation

The group explored attested, privacy-preserving monitoring as a future capability — for example, an encrypted closed-loop system that attests that work or review occurred and then deletes the underlying data, posting only the attestation to the chain, in contrast to centralized surveillance systems that retain and expose footage. The team discussed returning data control and value to the user (a Web3 "your data is your data" model), and noted enabling technologies such as event-based/neuromorphic cameras that preserve anonymity while allowing validation. The team agreed this was a digression from the immediate need and recommitted to defining a simple, clear core value proposition and entry point.
