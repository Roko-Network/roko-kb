# Business Meeting Summary

**Date:** December 23, 2025

---

## Purpose & Governance Overview

The meeting brought a financial advisor into the conversation to begin shaping an investor pitch and to understand Roko's current structure. The infrastructure lead walked through the present governance model, described as fluid and still largely centralized. Today, a small set of active community members vote through a centralized forum on proposals to fund operating expenses, hardware investment, and legal costs, on a one-token-one-vote basis with a historically high approval rate. The team noted that high approval may partly reflect speculative holders rather than deep technical understanding. The stated goal is to transition toward a decentralized model in which validators running network hardware become the primary voters, with existing holders able to delegate their voting power to trusted validators.

## Legal Structure & Decentralization Timeline

The team reiterated its two-entity structure: a foundation representing the DAO and holding assets, and a corporation that executes contracts and developer distributions on the foundation's behalf, with a multi-signature wallet controlling treasury movements. The infrastructure lead noted that, on paper, they remain the ultimate beneficiary today — a centralization and liability concern the team wants to resolve by formally establishing token holders as beneficiaries once a decentralized governance system is in place. There was a difference of opinion on timing: one view favored giving the L1 at least a year in public operation — through certification audits and back-testing — before launching public on-chain governance, treating that period as the window to develop the on-chain proposal-and-execution software (substrate pallets) that would automate treasury actions. The team agreed that releasing governance documents prematurely was a concern and that low voter participation and limited community technical understanding are real obstacles in Web3 governance, making a cautious, well-founded structure preferable. Delegated/democratic staking — letting holders delegate votes to more informed validators — was discussed as a promising mechanism.

## Market Positioning & The "Last Mile"

The advisor probed Roko's differentiation. The team positioned the project as a hybrid analog-blockchain primitive: the blockchain layer validates and routes payment transactions while the timing layer delivers a practical, certified PTP timing service. The team pushed back on a framing that there are few timing alternatives, noting that backup-timing is a fast-growing sector with multiple players and significant government and financial-institution investment. Roko's distinct opportunity was framed as the "last mile" — bringing high-stratum, near-clock timing precision into front offices, labs, and homes rather than only data centers (where timing equipment is already commonplace). The team described an example of moving from coarse public-grade timing accuracy to centimeter-level and nanosecond-precision service at the edge, potentially meeting regulatory requirements for financial transactions. Roko's claimed differentiators included hardware attestation and continuity of time anchored to the blockchain ledger.

## Timing Service Partnership

The team explained that Roko is built on two foundations: the open-source time-appliance protocol (OCP TAP) where the academic and developer community lives, and the offering of a London-based timing service provider that developed an amendment enabling a distributed, non-hierarchical concept of time sync. The team described this provider as central to scaling — potentially supplying master-clock infrastructure, network certification, and international regional certifications — and discussed financing their expertise to articulate the PTP portion of the roadmap. The team indicated it has already purchased several clocks and a master clock from this provider and expects a functional test infrastructure by the end of January, and estimated that global service coverage could be achieved with on the order of low-eight-figure infrastructure spend, with clocks placed in roughly a dozen strategically and geopolitically chosen, legally favorable data-center sites.

## Investor Framing

The advisor emphasized translating the technology into investor-comprehensible value: a blockchain-primitive protocol whose nanosecond-precision, trustworthy timing makes traditional audit functions unnecessary, positioning Roko as a step beyond existing cloud compute offerings. The team agreed the near-term plan is to take a deeper dive on what Roko core versus partner-provided services should be, engage the timing partner for explicit infrastructure and service cost figures, and convene a follow-up session with the broader advisory group to translate the vision into a pitch.
