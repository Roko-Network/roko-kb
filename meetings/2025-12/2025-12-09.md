# Development & Strategy Meeting Summary

**Date:** December 9, 2025

---

## Admin Platform & Validator Onboarding

The infrastructure lead reported that the administrative platform for validator onboarding is being deployed this week. The platform connects to the chain, tracks chain status and the temporal time window automatically, and surfaces validator states and beacon information for debugging. Creating a new validator is fully automated: a single action generates the keys, sets the validator's balance, activates it, and returns a short set of run instructions so operators do not need to work through the full documentation. A faucet feature lets users request additional tokens.

During deployment the team uncovered a bug in how administrative (sudo-key) transactions interacted with the new temporal transaction system. Transactions lacking certain fields were being downgraded to normal transactions, which stripped required admin flags and broke the sudo key. The issue was diagnosed and fixed. The test chain has run to roughly 375,000 blocks without consensus issues; a single cloud instance running five validators was observed to be RAM-constrained, with usage measured at approximately 350 MB per validator — a useful baseline for future capacity planning.

## Temporal Transactions

Work on the time-ordering system continues. The team plans to proceed with the time RPC as the authoritative time source while also embedding beacons in transactions, so the beacons can later be evaluated as a potential replacement. A flag was added allowing the time RPC to be disabled — via the sudo key or community vote — in favor of beacons alone, but the assessment is that beacons and the alternative fallbacks are not yet robust enough and still leave attack vectors. The time RPC remains preferred because it is fast, provable on-chain (so it need not be trusted), and keeps the chain lightweight. Backdating prevention remains the hardest open problem, since the only rigorous defense is witnesses, which are difficult in a gossip network.

A remaining issue concerns transaction skipping during block production. Because each node may hold a slightly different mempool, a block producer could omit a temporal transaction that validators expect, causing the block to be rejected. The proposed solution piggybacks on the beacon system using a "magic number" mechanism that propagates faster than transactions themselves, signaling that unseen transactions exist. This work was nearly complete and will be finished as testnet validators come online. It is not required for initial testnet play, where the team is not actively probing for attack vectors.

## Marketing & Community

The team discussed an expanded marketing push tied to the new public-facing assets, including a weekly newsletter and broader presence across channels. A contributor offered to take ownership of LinkedIn outreach, motivated by feedback that the platform is an effective channel for sourcing investor and partnership contacts; the broader marketing plan is being documented so responsibilities can be distributed. The team also discussed warming up to regular live sessions on X (Twitter) — informal, authenticity-first Q&A — targeting a first session on a Friday afternoon. One member noted an emerging relationship with an AI and security-focused content collective whose larger channels could eventually amplify Roko announcements once arrangements are confirmed.

## Partnership & Business Development

A team member described an informal connection with the head of R&D at a major industrial tool manufacturer and proposed drafting a short write-up on a manufacturing vertical — quality-assurance monitoring, equipment failure detection, and oil/gas pipeline monitoring — as potential applications of Roko's technology. The team noted that OCP TAP compatibility means Roko provides improvements on existing systems rather than requiring rip-and-replace, lowering the barrier to industrial adoption.

## Decentralized Manufacturing Concept

An extended discussion explored a longer-term concept: using Roko's attestation technology as the trust layer for decentralized manufacturing of sub-assemblies. The vision pairs on-device cameras and sensors with blockchain-attested "proof of moment" timestamps to create a tamper-evident audit trail for distributed production and quality assurance — running entirely on-premise to address privacy concerns. The conversation connected this to broader themes of media authentication (proving that images or video were captured at a specific verifiable time and place) and a possible standards-based approach to associating attested media with on-chain records. The team agreed any such concept needs concrete demo applications before it is pitched, and that the recent dissolution of a robotics community had freed talent and open-source resources worth monitoring, though no outreach was committed.

## Funding & Treasury

A contributor presented a proposal to formalize ongoing engagement via a flat monthly retainer covering both time and the servers and network infrastructure they operate. The team acknowledged the value but flagged that the treasury would need to reserve funds for foreseeable new-year costs such as audits. The group agreed to fulfill one month of the request to stabilize the near term, consider a modest end-of-year distribution for contributors, and revisit the arrangement month-to-month in the new year. The discussion underscored that securing investment is now the central priority, with several leads under evaluation — including potential grants and an investment connection in the UAE — none of which provide immediate funds. The consensus was to keep the team together month-to-month while accelerating investment outreach, and to enter the new year with working demos to build investor confidence.
