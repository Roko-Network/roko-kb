# Development Meeting Summary

**Date:** May 26, 2026

---

## Explorer Status

The new explorer version is about to be pushed to production. It tracks both substrate and EVM transactions, carries most of the Polkadot.js feature set, and lets users interact with both sides of the Roko chain. The explorer was characterized as a non-critical component, but meaningful code progress has been made. On the nodes, no further changes were made since the last indexing update; the network reported full stability with blocks still being produced across four validators and time quality performing well. The front end can be re-skinned away from the default Polkadot look toward Roko branding relatively quickly, beginning with colors and logo.

## Rewards and Fee System Overview

The bulk of the meeting was a structured walkthrough of the reward and fee system, which is central to incentivizing network participation. Two distinct value paths were presented: transaction and gas fees (native Roko) and staking inflation (Power Roko). Inflation is minted per era rather than per block, with a configurable annual rate that fluctuates between a floor of roughly 2.5 percent and a ceiling of 10 percent depending on the proportion of staked tokens; both the floor and ceiling are themselves adjustable. Staked rewards accrue in Power Roko, which is bonded and requires an unbonding period of around two weeks before it can be unlocked. Power Roko is backed one-to-one by native Roko at all times; substrate mints the native coin in many places across different pallets, so the implementation tracks every minting path to keep the backing balanced — a property the team reported as stable for five months.

## Fee Distribution Splits

Transaction and gas fees are paid in native Roko, distributed every block, and split three ways. The default split discussed allocates 20 percent to the treasury, 50 percent to the block author, and 30 percent to timestampers. These values are configurable and can be adjusted through governance. The block-author share was viewed as difficult to push below roughly 50 percent, while the treasury and timestamper shares carry more room to move; more aggressive splits weighting timestampers heavily (for example, 80 percent) were noted as possible, to emphasize that timestamping is the primary value-generating activity. The timestamper share is not split per timestamp but is quality-weighted: validators with better time quality earn a larger portion of the pooled fees, including a share redistributed from underperformers, creating a competitive dynamic within that allocation. If no validator is vested to timestamp a block, the timestamper share reverts to the block author. The block-author role itself is a simple round-robin among eligible staked validators.

## Tuning the Reward Values

The team discussed when and why these values might change. The consensus was that the splits function more as a directional signal of what the chain values than as a precise financial calculation, since the eventual number of validators, number of timestampers, and token value are all unknown and cannot yet be modeled. Adjustments were expected to be rare, heavily debated, and most likely concentrated in the first year or two as the network matures. One illustrative path discussed: if the treasury's share of supply grows past a majority threshold, the treasury split could be reduced and reallocated proportionally to block authors and timestampers. The idea of an agent-driven mechanism that dynamically adjusts reward values based on network needs was raised but set aside as not appropriate at this stage.

## Tokenomics Research and Hardware Tiers

A forward-design tokenomics document was referenced, drawing on analysis of allocation discrepancies across a wide range of established tokens and failure cases, and covering topics such as staking multipliers, governance decay, and buyback-and-burn from treasury revenue. The possibility of hardware-tiered reward multipliers — higher emissions for validators running more precise clocks — was discussed but largely set aside, on the reasoning that better timing hardware should already translate into winning more quality-weighted timestamping rewards without a separate multiplier mechanism.

## Validator Hardware

Deployment of the new validator with the improved timing hardware remains in progress and has been a struggle; no firm launch date was committed, with the work described as ongoing and expected to come online soon.

## Documentation, Marketing, and Rollout

With the tokenomics direction taking shape, the team discussed translating internal documentation into public-facing material and planning a phased, multi-week rollout covering onboarding and the current tokenomics. Marketing support is being prepared, with several contributors trained over the prior months and already producing materials; existing branding and marketing assets were noted as living in dedicated repositories, including a separate shareable media repository for partners and journalists containing branding kits, colors, and logos. The team also discussed how long a publicly running testnet should operate before progressing toward mainnet, treating that timeline as dependent on data gathered once external node operators participate.

## Funding Note

A high-level funding update was shared: the liquid treasury balance was reported in the low tens of thousands of dollars, with remaining holdings non-liquid. The team flagged that meeting recurring monthly commitments could become difficult by the end of June, with the expectation that recurring revenue and potential funding would help close the gap. This is noted at a high level only.
